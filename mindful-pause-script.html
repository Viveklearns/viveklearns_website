Mindful Pause

A script for when the urge arrives

begin
Tap to start a breathing guide · 4 in, 6 out
  1. 1 Stop. Hands off.
    Physically remove your hands from the keyboard or phone. Place them palms-down on your thighs or flat on the desk. This breaks the autopilot loop before it starts. "I notice the pull. I'm choosing to pause."
  2. 2 Name what's happening.
    Silently label the feeling. Not a story, just a word: arousal, wanting, restlessness. Naming it moves you from being inside the urge to observing it from the outside. "Wanting is here. I see it."
  3. 3 Three slow breaths.
    Breathe in through your nose for 4 counts. Out through your mouth for 6 counts. Feel the air at the tip of your nostrils. Let the exhale be soft and complete. Repeat three times. Use the breathing circle above if it helps. "In… 2… 3… 4… Out… 2… 3… 4… 5… 6…"
  4. 4 Feel, don't follow.
    Drop your attention into your body. Where is the sensation? Warmth in the chest, tension in the belly, energy somewhere lower? Just feel the raw sensation without building a story around it. Without the mental images, the feeling is just a feeling. It's manageable. "This is sensation. It's not a command."
  5. 5 Surf the wave.
    The urge is a wave. It will build, crest, and fall on its own — usually within 10 to 20 minutes — if you don't feed it. You don't need to fight it or push it away. Just stay still and watch. Notice the intensity rising, the peak, and then the slow fade. Each wave you ride makes the next one smaller. "This will pass. It always does."
  6. 6 Redirect with purpose.
    Ask yourself: "What do I actually want to be doing right now?" Then do that one thing. Stand up. Walk to another room. Get water. Change your physical context — the urge is tied to the environment. Even a small shift breaks the spell. "I choose what I do next."

The feeling arose, and you stayed present.
That is enough. That is the practice.
